Naming should mean something

Auto-cannibalistic fiction,

labyrinthine consciousness,

meta-narcissism.

“

Vulgar darkness. I see no end in this empty room.

This is just the start.

Vulgar darkness. I see no end in this empty room. Go on, try, shout! My strength is running out. I killed myself trying to catch up with Δ, to know her walls, to feel the imaginary cracks. We parted ways long ago — she moved away. She wanted so much…

I will never leave this darkness. What keeps me here is hatred for creativity. Silence is the best music. Funny — it is simultaneously punishment and eternal happiness. Every word of mine is an attempt to bring her back. But she goes further and further into the immeasurable. From my static space into a new, jelly‑like active one. I have no goals. I am here because I burn with my own powerlessness. My chains are my best friends. They tell me that I am right and she is not. True, but I don't believe them. Write no matter what! — that's what I hear from them. Every letter makes me want to stop. Hey, I do love you! Please answer… Silence is the best music.

I am detached from my future. Or, perhaps, locked in the past. A miniature dot as a symbol of the absence of uniqueness. These punctuation marks bring me closer to her. Guess — was I standing still or moving forward: did this puddle appear here or did I come to it? Lower your gaze, look at yourself! My strength is running out. Δ, doesn't it seem to you that I am close to you? Painful. Hellishly unpleasant, but I have come to terms with these chains. She is moving away again. Who is she, anyway? A part of me. My consciousness. My inner desires, the abstraction of my creative life. I love her because she does not wear chains. This slave's décor is alien to her. Once, she too felt the cracks of this world.

I found the strength to stand up. And take a step. This is not a chase; this is a war. A war with myself. We are all human, we communicate in one language to understand each other. But the need to be understood is in itself a weakness of humanity. And this is not a striving for empathy on the part of people; it too is merely a part of the chain. It's as if I live in a two‑dimensional space, shouting to her, but she, there in her three‑dimensional one, does not hear. Did I step into the puddle because I went further, or because it appeared here? In the reflection, a desire flickered. My desire to be with her. She is perfect — so to speak, "written on her forehead" what she wants to say. But it is done in such a perfect form, on a face beautiful to the point of ugliness.

I sighed. The last thing one could hope for was God's help. I fell to my knees to pray for Δ. Nonsense. But suppose I succeeded. He would hear me. And then what?!… I see her Δ face, Δ lips. I am drawn to her Δ attire, her hair soft as Δ. Every step of mine makes me more perfect. The cracks break, and I leave my native home. And now we embrace — what next? There are two options, and they are opposite: either nothing, or nothing. Period.

We move on from my abstract silence to painful steps. It's so dirty. When the sunbeams blinded me, the patterns of the dream that had visited me during that mute night began to take shape on the ceiling. I was in an infinitely long study, whose side walls were so close that they seemed to be drawing nearer and would sooner or later crush me. The light remained dim: I don't remember if it had a source? I was surrounded by a company of friends, all faces familiar. Perhaps, if I had walked a little further, I would have seen other people. But I stayed where I was — why would I need that? And then, a serious man approached me. I immediately recognized him as our professor.

"Your last work is magnificent; you have high potential for becoming recognizable," he said, handing me the work he had been grading the other day. I turned to my friends and began to complain about the title of my own work: "Professional story, seriously? That's maximally unprofessional." From the very first line, I didn't like my work. And it had always been that way. Every author writes their stories in the moment: for some, that moment is fleeting; for others, it rivals eternity. Words cannot convey the feeling a writer experiences upon finishing their book. Chapters, tomes, acts. It's all artificial. But when the creator puts down that firm final period, before his soul, the complete picture of that spectacle he wanted to paint finally takes shape. For a time, he feels happiness. The happiness of freedom. An interesting thing, by the way. The author of a story experiences it twice: before and after writing. In one case, he sees a million ways to begin it; in the second, only one single one, showing how much he loved the idea. His words, limited by ignorance of perfection, have formed a beautiful, finished framework for future stories.

Time passes. The writer takes on a new story. Again the feeling of freedom, again he imagines images coming to life within the absurd lines of text. That final period wasn't long in coming. The author is pleased; he looks at his tale. But he notices that atop the first framework, he has erected another. He hasn't moved to the next stage, hasn't become more flexible. The creator is bewildered. But he doesn't give up. Time to tackle a third work. This time, he feels that this will be the one — the one that will bring him fame or absolute satisfaction. He has sketched out a completely different, unique world. Multifaceted characters, sensitive images, and astringent constructions. His tale exudes beauty. Honestly, anyone would look at this third framework and say that it is so. But the creator is incapable of building anything greater within his own petty conception. He loves his stories in the moment. All because these stories are merely fleeting letters sent one way, in the hope that sooner or later, they will be opened.

The professor praised my work, but I clearly didn't care for it. While I was thinking globally, my friends, it seemed, had taken on the next story. He asked us to write a story about childhood. No additional information, only the title — "The Bicycle and the Clouds." It turned out this was a story by a mythical author, the text of which no one can find. I began to write. May mine not also become mythically lost…

I picked up my pen, having written nothing yet. And that makes it so interesting — I am free! It could be something absolutely insane; it could be just amusing. But in any case, once I begin, I will understand that I am limited by what is written. The infinite space for creativity instantly turns into a room where all the walls are bound by enormous chains. And I am somewhere in the center. This isn't it… Again, not it… Write one word; the second must fit it… Circle after circle. I decided to pour my anger into the text…

I felt as if I had fallen into a cycle of memories. Once, he told me: "I think, in the future, I'll be able to submit your book for printing…" And I bet my classmates that it would be so. The bet, needless to say, I lost. Yes. I never even tried. The notebook was empty. Anyway, my childhood stories were far too naively cartoonish. Invented super-powered characters making universes tremble! No complex words or surprising narration. I looked into the small mirror. It was there that I saw myself peeling the bark off sticks to chop bushes near the abandoned lot. It seems I had already made a full circle. But that was only the beginning.

I remembered how naive I was. It seems everything changed rather abruptly. Either me, or the world around me. It feels like just yesterday I was gobbling down that huge baguette dry and telling everyone how delicious it was. Then I'd fill my mouth with raisins and watch whatever was on TV. I kept rolling. A circle… As big as that cake the day before my birthday. Am I to blame that they always taste better right before? Probably, it needs to set, but as a kid, you couldn't care less about all that. Potatoes. Yes, just normal, fried. But for some reason, that smell of oil really stuck with me. I was watching TV then, some commercial. So many things that make me who I am. But do I need all of it?

The little wheels were already wobbling, but that doesn't mean I'll stop spinning. This moment will be remembered, too. And let it be remembered as the link connecting all the others. I remember being happy about the evening buns in kindergarten. Funny, I've hardly eaten any since then. I rarely attended kindergarten. I didn't like napping at noon. All the kids would go for quiet time, but I would just stare at the ceiling. The pedals became harder to turn… Friend, I remember swinging with you on the swings. The button phone and the music you managed to download. Even earlier, they were just preinstalled ringtones. I love swings. Because of you. But now I feel lonely riding them alone.

The wind began to blow in my direction, but I kept striving forward. Forgive me, friend. We were close friends, but we spontaneously stopped communicating. It wasn't my choice, but I wouldn't say I was hurt. That kid forgot you quickly. I had already ridden over the gravel, but the bike kept sailing on. We came from the same kindergarten. We weren't the same age, but we understood each other easily. You lived nearby, so I'd run into you occasionally. At some point, I stopped saying hello to you. Even though we're made of the same stuff. And your name… I fell in love with it far too late.

The kids from the street gathered in the sandbox, each doing their own thing. But I was lost in my own thoughts. It often seemed to me that something was happening on the clouds. I looked at the sky and saw the extraterrestrial atmosphere of my own imagination…

"Perhaps it is for sale after all?" inquired a man in a black hat. His hair noticeably covered his eyes, but that didn't stop him from wearing a monocle. "No, how many times must I say it? Nothing here is for sale," replied a girl indignantly, who looked more like a princess. A thin dress and her velvety eyes… No, not like that. The man with the monocle couldn't have seen them. Why did I think of that?

"I myself wouldn't mind taking it, you know." "Oh, just look, my dear lady. He's simply begging to come with me…" A woman joined the conversation. She looked completely different. Something unusually alien, yet achingly familiar. Comparable, perhaps, to cartoons about cyberpunk robots or books about the future. Although that's nonsense; they'd surely laugh at such a comparison with those worlds. Her beautiful face was accentuated by red glasses. Some small pieces of metal stuck out of them. "The animals here are exhibits. There's already a crisis in the world right now; I won't allow you to buy anyone here," she said. "Madam, you've frightened me. I've never seen such an unusual outfit…" the man uttered. "Forget about me. Enjoy these lovely creatures," said the girl in the electronic glasses, "they are the only thing that unites time into one whole."

The fluffy floor occasionally deformed. But that only created an atmosphere of eternal motion into the distance. No one knew where the wind was blowing or where they were flying next. Everyone was looking at the little dog, around whom this conversation had begun. Only silence and the acceptance of natural beauty. The little dog radiated life. It could offer its paw to the princess, gaze joyfully at the man. It could try to comfort the woman, who had just shed a tear, or simply busy itself with its own affairs. "I always wanted a dog. But in our castle (a one-room apartment), it was forbidden!" a girl interrupted the silence. "In my estate (a house on the edge of a village), I already have a faithful dog. I dreamed of finding him a cheerful friend." "In our world, dogs have become merely a beautiful fairy tale. On the streets walk other dogs, which we feed with electricity (reeking gasoline)," recounted the woman.

It was one place. An exhibition of animals on soft clouds. But for some reason, everyone saw what was happening differently. The man saw only this little dog and only these people. They stood on a carpet floating in the clouds, as beautiful as the one in his estate. The princess's eyes, however, darted everywhere: she saw hundreds of other little dogs and was already tired of looking at them. Each was beautiful in its own way. She could pet each one. The woman saw a family of dogs. They were eating food from a bowl and sharing it with each other. But everyone saw the beauty of the sky, the penetrating sunbeams. And it was beautiful. Time could last here for an eternity, but human resources are limited. It was time to slowly descend back down.

The clouds formed three staircases: for the man, it was a large circular one; for the girl, a slippery slide; and for the woman, an ordinary one that you had to descend on your own two feet. All the characters said their goodbyes and returned to their worlds. The mysterious man with the monocle turned out to be a poor, abandoned old man whose best friend — his dog — had recently died. The pompous princess turned out to be a girl whose parents are afraid to let her out of the apartment. She has to go to the window to look at the dogs. And the wise woman from the future returned to her artificial, overly automated world.

The kids from the street gathered in the sandbox, each doing their own thing. And I began to reflect on the exhibition. An exhibition of animals on the clouds. For me, it was normal to imagine the people from there and to look for a staircase up.
