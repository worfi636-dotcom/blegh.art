Second single from the album The Here And Now

This was the second single, and it keeps the atmospheric feel going. Like the opener, it starts with a riff that feels almost solo – clean and ringing. Once again, Architects play with tempo and mood. The verses are quieter, with light guitar in the background. The pre-chorus pulls you in, and then the chorus hits – big, anthemic, the kind you want to shout along to. By the time the outro comes around, with everything crashing together, it leaves you completely drained.

The message is clear: learn to live... without them. Who are "them"? Anyone who drags you down. A bad relationship, false friends, people you feel stuck with even though they're no good for you. The song says you have to let them go. Only then can you make it through the rain. There's a clever line: "I never paid much attention to them." It sounds like a lie we tell ourselves. We're only human – cutting people off is hard. But pretending there's no problem doesn't help either. Then there's the classic line: "The grass is greener on the other side, so don't give up on life." It's a smart play on words. Other people's lives might look better from the outside, but that's usually an illusion. So we keep living. We don't give up.

As the second single, this track holds onto the album's moody side. A lot of fans didn't love The Here And Now, but if this was the music Architects truly wanted to make, they should have kept doing it without them, without the fans who couldn't accept it. In that sense, this song connects to their later work like Little Wonder (lying to yourself that everything's fine) and Seeing Red (doing what pleases "them").
